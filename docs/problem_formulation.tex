\noindent We consider a cooperative \textbf{Multi-Robot Coverage Path Planning
(MR-CPP)} problem: a team of robots must physically cover every reachable cell
of an initially unknown indoor floor (\emph{exploratory coverage}) while
avoiding walls, other robots and, optionally, pedestrians. Each robot keeps its
own persistent map belief and merges it with teammates when they are within
communication range, an approach inspired by \cite{xiao2020a_distributed}.
Velocity commands are produced by a policy trained with Multi-Agent
Reinforcement Learning (MAPPO) under centralized training with decentralized
execution (CTDE). The policy is first initialized by Imitation Learning (IL)
from a Boustrophedon Cellular Decomposition (BCD) expert, and a classical
A*{}+DWA fallback takes over when the learned behaviour stops making progress,
inspired by \cite{carvalho2025deep}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Fixed requirements.} Individual map beliefs, intermittent
map exchange, perfect odometry, random initial positions, 2D LiDAR sensing,
continuous actions, MARL with CTDE, emergent (implicit) coordination and a
fallback controller. \textbf{Modelling choices.} Physical floor coverage, known
initial poses and homogeneous differential-drive robots.

%==========================================================================
\section{Environment and robots}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]

\item \textbf{Workspace and layout distribution.}
The floor is a bounded $12\times 8$~m rectangle subdivided by recursive space
partitioning into rooms, corridors and doorways (Fig.~\ref{fig:floor}).
Interior walls are grid-aligned and occupy a full cell band. Every episode
samples a layout from a bank of 64 procedurally generated maps, to favour
generalization; held-out map seeds are used for evaluation. The floor
dimensions are fixed, but the layout is unknown to the robots.

\item \textbf{Continuous motion, discrete coverage.}
Robots move in continuous space, while mapping and coverage use a common
square grid with resolution $\delta=0.5$~m ($24\times16$ cells). A free cell
is \emph{covered} as soon as the robot disc overlaps it (a neighbouring cell
counts once the disc enters it by $0.8\,r$). Coverage is permanent within an
episode. The episode ends when all reachable free cells are covered or after
$T_{\max}=4000$ steps ($400$~s).

\item \textbf{Robot team.}
The team is $\mathcal I=\{1,\dots,N\}$ with $N=3$. Robots are homogeneous:
identical sensing, motion limits and capabilities. Initial positions are
sampled at random among collision-free free cells.

\item \textbf{Kinematics.}
Each robot is a disc of radius $r=0.2$~m with pose $q_i=(x_i,y_i,\theta_i)$
and differential-drive (unicycle) kinematics
\[
 \dot x_i=v_i\cos\theta_i,\qquad
 \dot y_i=v_i\sin\theta_i,\qquad
 \dot\theta_i=\omega_i,
\]
integrated with step $\Delta t=0.1$~s.

\item \textbf{Continuous action space.}
At each step the policy outputs $a_i\in(-1,1)^2$ (tanh-squashed Gaussian),
mapped to
\[
 v_i\in[0,v_{\max}],\quad \omega_i\in[-\omega_{\max},\omega_{\max}],
 \qquad v_{\max}=1~\text{m/s},\ \omega_{\max}=1~\text{rad/s}.
\]
MARL approaches to MR-CPP with continuous actions are currently a gap in the
literature.

\item \textbf{Collisions.}
Collisions are \emph{soft}: a move into a wall, a robot or a pedestrian is
rejected, the robot is penalized, and the episode continues.

\item \textbf{Pedestrians (optional).}
Pedestrians walk at $0.5$~m/s along straight segments of random heading and
length, and stop when a robot is too close. They are disabled in the current
IL+RL configuration and enabled for robustness experiments.

\item \textbf{Perfect sensing and common frame.}
Each robot knows its initial global pose and integrates perfect odometry, so
its trajectory is known in a common global frame without drift. Each robot
carries a 2D LiDAR with $70$ rays over $360^\circ$ and $5$~m range, used both
to discover the floor and to sense obstacles and other agents.

\end{enumerate}

%==========================================================================
\section{Map memory and communication}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},resume]

\item \textbf{Individual map belief.}
Robot $i$ keeps a persistent map $M_i$ that is initially empty and grows as
LiDAR rays discover new cells or teammates share theirs. Each cell is in one
of the states
\[
 \textit{unknown},\quad
 \textit{known--occupied},\quad
 \textit{known--free--uncovered},\quad
 \textit{known--free--covered}.
\]
A cell becomes \emph{known} when a LiDAR ray crosses it (or hits it, for
walls), but it becomes \emph{covered} only when the robot body passes over it:
discovering a cell is not covering it. Wall edges hit by LiDAR are also stored
as blocked graph edges. The memory is implemented as per-robot bitmaps
(\textit{known}, \textit{covered}, \textit{blocked edges}) with the same
union semantics as a sparse dictionary of cell keys.

\item \textbf{Communication.}
Two robots communicate when their distance is below
$R_{\mathrm{comm}}=3$~m. Connected robots merge their maps by logical OR
(known cells, covered cells and blocked edges), so after an exchange they
share the same belief. Exchange is simultaneous and single-hop at every step;
multi-hop propagation happens over successive steps. When disconnected, robots
keep sensing, mapping and acting independently, so redundant coverage is
possible.

\end{enumerate}

%==========================================================================
\section{Actor observation}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},resume]

\item \textbf{Observation frame.}
At each step robot $i$ builds, only from its own sensing and its own map:
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Pose}: $(x/W,\ y/H,\ \cos\theta,\ \sin\theta)$.
 \item \textbf{Last executed command}: $(v/v_{\max},\ \omega/\omega_{\max})$.
 \item \textbf{Robot id}.
 \item \textbf{Teammates in communication range}: up to $2$ slots
       $(\mathrm{id}_j,\ \Delta x_j,\ \Delta y_j)$, zero when empty.
 \item \textbf{History}: validity flag and relative position of the
       previously visited cell, and the current sweep direction.
 \item \textbf{LiDAR}: $70$ normalized ranges.
 \item \textbf{Local map crop} (from \cite{hasan2025efficient}): a $5\times5$
       grid-aligned window centred on the robot, with three Boolean channels
       \textit{occupied}, \textit{covered}, \textit{known}. Cells beyond the
       floor boundary are marked as occupied and known.
 \item \textbf{Full personal map}: the whole $24\times16$ belief with five
       channels (\textit{occupied}, \textit{covered}, \textit{known},
       \textit{self position}, \textit{teammates in range}). Unknown
       geometry and out-of-range teammates are not exposed.
\end{itemize}
No target cell, waypoint or route is given: the actor must choose where to go
from its memory and history.

\item \textbf{Distant information.}
In the IL+RL configuration distant knowledge is provided by the full personal
map, encoded by a CNN. The alternative \emph{distant summary} of
\cite{carvalho2025deep}, a one-cell ring that extends the crop to $7\times7$
and stores, in each direction, the average status of all cells beyond the
crop plus an extent channel, is also implemented and used in the baseline
configuration.

\item \textbf{Observation stacking.}
The last $5$ frames (current one included, $0.4$~s of history) are stacked in
time order and encoded with shared weights; missing frames at episode start are
zero-filled. A GRU actor is available as an alternative form of memory.

\end{enumerate}

%==========================================================================
\section{Learning: MAPPO with CTDE}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},resume]

\item \textbf{Decision model.}
The task is a cooperative Dec-POMDP. Each actor acts on its own observation
history; parameters are shared among the homogeneous robots, while runtime
memory is not.

\item \textbf{Actor network.}
LiDAR is encoded by a circular 1D CNN, the personal map by a 2D CNN, and the
features of the five frames are concatenated with the vector and crop inputs
and passed to an MLP (two layers of $128$ units). The output is the mean of a
tanh-squashed Gaussian with a state-independent, bounded standard deviation.

\item \textbf{Centralized critic (privileged global state).}
During training each robot has a value $V_i(s)$ computed from an
agent-centred global state that actors cannot access. Each of the last $5$
frames contains:
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Grid channels}: walls, true team coverage, own position,
       teammates' positions, and a visit-count map for every robot.
 \item \textbf{Vector}: pose and velocity of all robots (and pedestrian
       positions, when present), previous visited cell of every robot, and a
       task context (episode time, revisit streak, stall counter, fallback
       state and goal, sweep direction and lane state, current revisit weight).
\end{itemize}
The critic is a 2D CNN followed by an MLP (two layers of $256$ units).

\item \textbf{PPO setup.}
Generalized advantage estimation ($\gamma=0.99$, $\lambda=0.95$), clipping
$0.2$, entropy coefficient $0.01$, early stop of actor epochs at KL $0.02$,
Huber value loss, running observation normalization and reward scaling
$0.05$. Learning rates are $10^{-4}$ (actor) and $10^{-3}$ (critic) with
linear decay; $32$ environments $\times$ $128$ steps per update, $4$ epochs
and $8$ minibatches.

\end{enumerate}

%==========================================================================
\section{Reward function}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},resume]

\item \textbf{Per-robot reward.}
Every term uses only the robot's own knowledge, except coverage and completion,
which use the true team coverage. With $C\in[0,1]$ the team coverage
fraction:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.32\linewidth}p{0.6\linewidth}}
\toprule
\textbf{Term} & \textbf{Value} \\
\midrule
New coverage & $+10\,(1+2C)$ per newly covered cell (worth more late in the episode) \\
Sequential coverage & $+6$ if the new cell is adjacent to the previous one;
  $+4$ more if it continues the same direction \\
Boustrophedon lane change & $+8$ after a straight run of $\ge 2$ cells, a
  one-cell perpendicular shift, and a new cell in the opposite direction \\
Progress towards work & $+2$ per cell of geodesic approach to the nearest
  believed-uncovered cell (routes through known free cells; exploration
  frontiers when no known work remains) \\
Loitering & $-0.2\,(1-\text{progress}/\text{full-speed progress})$ per step
  without new coverage \\
Off-axis motion & $-0.5\,(e/45^\circ)^2\,\|v\|/v_{\max}$, with $e$ the angle
  between the motion and the nearest axis \\
Spreading & $-0.5\,\max(0,1-d/2.5~\text{m})$ per teammate in communication range \\
Re-coverage & $-w(\phi)\cdot\min(k,3)$ for the $k$-th consecutive entry into a
  covered cell, with $w(\phi)=6\,[0.025+0.975(1-\phi)^2]$ and
  $\phi=\max(C,\,t/T_{\max})$ \\
Time & $-0.02$ per step \\
Collision & $-2$ wall, $-5$ robot, $-10$ pedestrian \\
Fallback activation & $-10$ once per activation \\
Completion & $+200\,(1+\text{remaining time fraction})$, shared by the team \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\item \textbf{Rationale.}
The sequential and lane-change terms reward Boustrophedon-like sweeps; the
axis term favours horizontal and vertical lanes. The re-coverage penalty is
high at the beginning and decays as coverage or time advances, since near the
end of the episode crossing covered cells is unavoidable. Progress is
potential-based (it telescopes to zero over loops), so a robot whose area is
finished is pushed towards remaining work rather than idling. Coordination is
implicit: there is no room assignment or goal negotiation.

\end{enumerate}

%==========================================================================
\section{Imitation Learning initialization}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},resume]

\item \textbf{Motivation.}
Systematic sweeping is hard to discover from rewards alone. The actor is
therefore pre-trained by imitating a BCD expert, then fine-tuned with MAPPO.

\item \textbf{BCD expert (teacher, training only).}
Offline, for each map, the free space is decomposed into rectangles, each
swept with Boustrophedon lanes along its longer side; the rectangles are
chained into a single ordered tour of all free cells. Online, the target of
each robot is the nearest cell that is uncovered in the robot's own memory,
ties broken by tour order (the next cell of the lane, or of the next lane at a
lane end), so the decision depends only on information the actor also
observes. A feedback law
\[
 \omega=\omega_{\max}\tanh(k\,e),\qquad
 v=v_{\mathrm{cruise}}\,\max(\cos e,0)^{p}\,\mathrm{clearance}(\text{LiDAR})
\]
tracks a waypoint on the shortest path to the target ($e$ heading error,
$v_{\mathrm{cruise}}=0.8$~m/s). Unsafe commands are replaced by DWA, and a
blocked robot yields to a lower-id teammate. The decomposition uses the true
layout, so the expert is privileged and exists only at training time.

\item \textbf{Behaviour cloning with DAgger.}
For $300$ iterations, rollouts are collected in which each robot executes the
expert action with probability $\beta$ and the actor's action otherwise
($\beta$ decays from $1$ to $0$ in $150$ iterations); the expert labels every
visited state. The actor mean is trained on a replay of the last $32$ rollouts
with a weighted MSE
\[
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{BC}}=\mathbb E\!\left[w\,\bigl\|\tanh(\mu_\theta(o))-a^{\mathrm{exp}}\bigr\|^2\right],
 \qquad w=1+2\min\bigl(\|a^{\mathrm{exp}}-a_{\mathrm{prev}}\|,1\bigr),
\]
which up-weights decision points (turns, lane shifts, stops). Only labels whose
expert target lies inside the actor's $5\times5$ crop are used, so the actor
is never asked to imitate information it cannot observe. The fallback is
disabled in this phase.

\item \textbf{Critic warm-up.}
With the actor frozen, the RL setup (reward, fallback, discount, GAE) runs for
$100$ updates and only the critic is trained, so that PPO starts from the
value of the policy it improves.

\item \textbf{RL fine-tuning.}
MAPPO resumes from the IL checkpoint with a pre-tanh exploration standard
deviation of $0.3$. An optional BC term towards the expert can be kept during
RL, decaying linearly to zero.

\end{enumerate}

%==========================================================================
\section{Fallback controller}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},resume]

\item \textbf{Trigger.}
Following the hybrid idea of \cite{carvalho2025deep}, a robot switches to the
fallback after $7$ consecutive entries into covered cells or $70$ steps
($7$~s) without covering a new cell.

\item \textbf{Planning and execution.}
A* selects the nearest reachable uncovered cell in the robot's own memory,
routing through known free cells and respecting remembered walls. DWA
($12$-step horizon, acceleration limits, current LiDAR clearance) follows the
route. If DWA stops making progress for $30$ steps, a precomputed sequence of
velocity commands ($\le 0.4$~m/s), checked against the latest LiDAR before each
command, drives the robot along the route. The fallback keeps control until
its goal cell is covered (by itself or a teammate), then returns control to the
policy. It never uses the true map.

\item \textbf{Consistency with learning.}
Steps controlled by the fallback are excluded from the PPO policy-gradient
and entropy terms, since they are not samples from the actor; the critic
still learns from all transitions. Collision-free fallback commands train the
actor through a separate imitation loss (coefficient $0.5$). Results are
reported both with the fallback (assisted) and without it (policy only).

\end{enumerate}

%==========================================================================
\section{Evaluation}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},resume]

\item \textbf{Metrics.}
On held-out layouts and seeds: success (full coverage) rate, robot--robot,
robot--wall and robot--pedestrian collision rates, timeout rate, completion
time of successful episodes, re-coverage ratio (cell visits / distinct covered
cells) and fallback usage. Policy-only and assisted runs are compared on the
same seeds.

\item \textbf{To be defined.}
Ablations on communication, personal map vs.\ distant summary, observation
stacking vs.\ GRU, IL initialization and fallback; generalization to other
team sizes, map sizes and pedestrian densities. Graph Attention Networks
\cite{velivckovic2017graph}, reported to improve coordination in multi-agent
exploration \cite{chiun2025marvel}, are a candidate for the actor--critic
architecture.

\end{enumerate}
